Election 2026 Policy Map

Under proportional representation your party vote helps decide which group of parties governs. This page shows the likely coalitions, where they agree and disagree, what each party's plans cost, and how the parties line up. Figures are as of 1 October 2026.

Who could form a government

Seats are calculated with the Sainte-Laguë method used by the Electoral Commission, from the average of the six most recent polls (27 July to 3 September 2026). Move a slider to test other results. A party needs 5 percent of the party vote or one electorate seat. 61 of 120 seats is a majority.

Party support over time

Party vote percentage. Dots are individual polls. The solid line is the trend estimated from this pollster's polls only, and the shaded band is its 95 percent range, which narrows where polls are frequent and widens between them. After the latest poll the line is dashed and the band widens to election day, showing how far support could drift if nothing else is known. The number beside each party is the trend at the latest poll, plus or minus its 95 percent range. The first point is the 2023 election result. The dashed grey line is the 5 percent threshold. Select a party name to hide or show it.

Method: a random walk model fitted with a Kalman filter and smoother, as used by poll aggregators (Jackman 2005, Australian Journal of Political Science). Each poll is assumed to survey about 1,000 people, with its sampling error widened by a design effect of 1.5 to allow for weighting. How quickly support drifts is estimated from the data for each party and pollster. The band covers sampling error only; error shared by all polls, such as people who refuse to answer, is not included.

How the parties line up

Left to right scores from the Manifesto Project, an academic project that codes every sentence of each party manifesto. The score is the share of sentences coded as right minus the share coded as left, so it runs from minus 100 to plus 100. The most recent New Zealand manifestos it has scored are from 2020. It does not cover Opportunity, and scores for 2023 and 2026 are not yet published.

Notes and sources

  • Seat projections ignore overhang seats and assume parties under 5 percent win no electorate unless the box is ticked. Te Pāti Māori won six Māori electorates in 2023, so it is ticked by default.
  • The coalition groups follow the parties' stated preferences: National, ACT and New Zealand First govern together now; Labour, Green and Te Pāti Māori have signalled they would work together. Opportunity has not committed to either side.
  • No position on this page is judged by its author. The issue grid shows the parties' own pledges. The line up uses Manifesto Project scores (dataset version 2026a).
  • Most cost figures come from the Taxpayers' Union "Bribe-o-meter". The Taxpayers' Union is a lobby group that favours lower spending, and its totals for some parties include items the parties dispute or have not campaigned on. Party figures are used where they exist.
